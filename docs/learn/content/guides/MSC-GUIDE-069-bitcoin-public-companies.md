# How Public Companies Participate in Bitcoin

## 1. Introductory deck

Public companies can participate in Bitcoin by holding bitcoin, mining it, building payment or infrastructure products, providing custody or market services, issuing financial products, or financing a corporate strategy around the asset. These activities create different risks, disclosures, and incentives. Owning shares in a company is not the same as owning its bitcoin, and no treasury strategy, product, executive, or shareholder receives authority over Bitcoin consensus.

## 2. Full article

A public company is a corporation whose securities trade in public markets and whose reporting obligations depend on its jurisdiction and listing structure. In the United States, many public companies file annual, quarterly, and current reports with the Securities and Exchange Commission. Those filings can reveal how a company describes its Bitcoin activity, how it accounts for assets and revenue, how it finances operations, and which risks its board and management disclose.

The phrase “a public company is in Bitcoin” is too broad to explain what the company actually does. One company may hold bitcoin as a treasury asset while its operating business remains unrelated. Another may earn revenue from mining, payments, custody, trading, software, or hardware. A trust or exchange-traded product may hold bitcoin for the benefit of its own shareholders under a contractual structure that differs from ordinary corporate equity. These arrangements should be evaluated separately.

This guide was researched on July 27, 2026. Holdings, debt balances, custody relationships, products, accounting treatment, regulatory obligations, executive roles, and operating metrics can change. Every dated example and filing must be renewed immediately before publication.

### Participation is a collection of different activities

Public-company participation can include:

- allocating corporate cash or financing proceeds to bitcoin;
- operating mining equipment and sites;
- selling payment, custody, brokerage, data, software, or hardware services;
- integrating bitcoin into an existing product;
- sponsoring or servicing a financial product that holds bitcoin;
- lending against, pledging, or otherwise using bitcoin in financing arrangements;
- accepting bitcoin from customers or paying suppliers;
- funding open-source work or standards efforts; and
- disclosing Bitcoin-related risks to shareholders.

A company can perform several of these activities at once. The categories still matter because each activity has a different connection to revenue, custody, capital needs, regulation, security, and Bitcoin’s technical system.

Holding bitcoin is not the same as operating a Bitcoin business. A company may acquire bitcoin without selling any Bitcoin-related product. Conversely, a company can provide Bitcoin infrastructure or services while holding little bitcoin on its own balance sheet. Describing both companies as “Bitcoin companies” can hide the more important question: where does the company’s economic exposure come from?

### Corporate treasury holdings

A treasury strategy uses company-controlled capital to acquire or retain bitcoin. The capital may come from operating cash flow, asset sales, common-stock issuance, preferred securities, convertible notes, secured or unsecured debt, or other financing. The resulting position belongs to the company or a controlled subsidiary under the disclosed legal and custody arrangement.

Strategy’s 2025 Form 10-K, accessed July 27, 2026, describes a business strategy centered on bitcoin and software, multiple custodians, equity and fixed-income financing, and material risks associated with bitcoin prices, liquidity, custody, leverage, and regulation. Its first-quarter 2026 Form 10-Q reported a dated holding figure as of April 26, 2026. Those filings establish what the company disclosed at those dates. They do not establish that the strategy will succeed, that the holdings remain unchanged, or that other companies should follow it.

A treasury position can affect a company’s balance sheet, earnings volatility, access to financing, tax position, liquidity, and risk profile. It can also create concentration: a decline in bitcoin’s market price may reduce reported asset value or make financing more difficult, while a rise may increase reported value without producing operating cash flow.

The analytical questions are therefore broader than “How many bitcoin does the company hold?” Readers should ask:

1. Which legal entity owns the asset?
2. How was the purchase financed?
3. Is any bitcoin pledged, lent, restricted, or committed as collateral?
4. Who controls the keys or authorizes transfers?
5. Which custodians or counterparties are involved?
6. What insurance, indemnification, or contractual limits apply?
7. Can the board change or end the strategy?
8. What happens if financing must be repaid during adverse market conditions?

### Shareholders own equity, not direct title to corporate bitcoin

A common share represents an equity interest in a corporation. It may carry voting rights and a residual claim on corporate assets and profits, subject to the company’s liabilities and governing documents. It ordinarily does not give the shareholder a specific onchain claim, withdrawal right, or private key for the company’s bitcoin.

This distinction matters when a company’s market value is discussed as though every share were a fixed amount of bitcoin. The company may have debt, preferred securities, cash, operating assets, taxes, contractual commitments, employee compensation, and other liabilities. It may issue or repurchase shares. It may buy, sell, pledge, or transfer bitcoin under board-approved policies. A shareholder’s economic exposure therefore depends on the entire capital structure and business, not only a reported bitcoin balance.

A bitcoin-holding trust or exchange-traded product also has its own legal structure. Shares may represent an interest in the trust, while creation, redemption, custody, fees, forks, and liquidation follow the governing documents. A trust share is still not the same thing as receiving the underlying private keys.

### Accounting treatment changes reported results

In December 2023, the Financial Accounting Standards Board issued ASU 2023-08 for certain crypto assets. For entities within its scope, the standard generally requires eligible crypto assets to be measured at fair value with changes recognized in net income, along with presentation and disclosure requirements. The standard is effective for fiscal years beginning after December 15, 2024, including interim periods, with early adoption permitted.

This changed the earlier U.S. accounting model under which many corporate bitcoin holdings were treated as indefinite-lived intangible assets and written down for impairment without recognizing upward market movements until sale. Under fair-value accounting, price movements can create substantial unrealized gains or losses in reported earnings.

Accounting treatment is not an economic guarantee. Fair value does not make bitcoin liquid in every market condition, eliminate custody risk, or provide cash for debt service. Readers should distinguish:

- the number of bitcoin disclosed;
- the accounting value at a reporting date;
- the historical purchase cost;
- unrealized changes in value;
- realized proceeds from a sale; and
- cash available for operations or financing obligations.

Companies outside U.S. generally accepted accounting principles may follow different standards. Jurisdiction, reporting framework, fiscal period, and adoption date must be stated.

### Mining as an operating business

A public mining company participates through industrial operations rather than only through asset allocation. It may acquire ASIC miners, develop sites, contract for power, join mining pools, manage firmware and fleets, and sell or retain mined bitcoin.

Mining filings often disclose hashrate, power capacity, fleet size, bitcoin production, energy expense, depreciation, hosting relationships, curtailment, and capital expenditures. Those metrics require definitions. Developed power capacity is not necessarily energized capacity. Nameplate hashrate is not necessarily deployed or realized hashrate. A production report is usually a company disclosure, not independent verification.

Mining companies may also retain bitcoin, issue equity or debt, enter equipment-financing arrangements, or pledge assets. The treasury and operating sides should be separated. A miner can produce bitcoin unprofitably, and a rising bitcoin balance does not by itself show that its sites, power contracts, or fleet are efficient.

Mining operations are examined in detail in MSC-GUIDE-071. The public-company question is how those operations appear in audited statements, risk factors, management discussion, and capital allocation.

### Payments, software, hardware, and infrastructure

Some public companies participate through products. Block’s 2025 Form 10-K describes consumer and merchant businesses as well as Bitcoin-related products and initiatives. Its disclosures distinguish company-owned bitcoin from bitcoin held for customers or used in product inventory and operations. Coinbase’s 2025 Form 10-K describes trading, custody, staking, stablecoin, subscription, institutional, and infrastructure activities across multiple crypto assets, with Bitcoin as one part of a broader company.

These filings illustrate why revenue must be attributed carefully. A company may report total revenue that includes many non-Bitcoin products. “Bitcoin-related revenue” may mean gross customer purchase volume, transaction revenue, hardware sales, subscription fees, custody fees, or another measure. Gross amounts can be much larger than the company’s net economics.

Product influence is also not consensus authority. A payment company can set deposit limits, transaction-review rules, withdrawal procedures, custody controls, supported address types, and product fees. Those are company policies. Bitcoin nodes still validate blocks and transactions according to the consensus rules their operators run.

### Custody and market services

Custody companies hold or help control keys for clients. Market-service companies may provide brokerage, execution, liquidity, settlement, or internal ledgers. A public company can perform these roles directly or through regulated subsidiaries.

Custody is not a single technical model. Assets may be held in segregated addresses, omnibus structures, cold storage, hot wallets, multiparty authorization systems, or arrangements involving subcustodians. Contractual terms may allocate responsibility differently for theft, operational error, insolvency, forks, airdrops, law-enforcement demands, and unsupported assets.

Coinbase’s 2025 Form 10-K describes separate operating entities, custody practices, client-asset risks, regulatory regimes, and internal controls. The filing is evidence of the company’s disclosures. It is not independent proof that losses cannot occur or that every control works under every condition.

When evaluating custody, readers should locate the actual responsible entity, governing agreement, jurisdiction, audit scope, insurance limits, key-control model, withdrawal process, and concentration risk. A brand name alone is not a custody architecture.

### Financial products create a separate wrapper

A public-market product can offer price exposure without giving investors direct custody. Spot bitcoin trusts and exchange-traded products typically involve a sponsor, trustee, administrator, custodian, authorized participants, market makers, and exchanges. Each role is contractual.

The iShares Bitcoin Trust ETF’s 2025 Form 10-K, for example, describes bitcoin custody, creation and redemption processes, service providers, fees, fork treatment, insurance limitations, and vendor risks. Those disclosures show how a product is operated. They do not convert the sponsor, custodian, or shareholders into Bitcoin protocol governors.

The wrapper creates risks that do not exist in direct self-custody, including sponsor decisions, service-provider failure, trading-market discounts or premiums, fees, redemption limits, and legal restrictions. Direct self-custody creates different risks, including key loss and operational error. Neither model should be described as universally safer without a defined threat model.

### Corporate financing can amplify both opportunity and risk

A company may raise capital because investors value its Bitcoin exposure. It may issue common shares, convertible debt, preferred stock, secured notes, or other instruments. The financing can increase the amount of bitcoin the company can acquire or the scale of an operating business. It can also create fixed obligations, dilution, refinancing risk, collateral requirements, and dependence on capital-market access.

Debt terms matter. A low coupon does not remove maturity risk. Convertible notes may dilute shareholders. Preferred securities may rank ahead of common equity. Secured borrowing may expose pledged assets. At-the-market stock issuance can raise cash while increasing share count.

A useful analysis therefore separates:

- operating cash generation;
- asset-price gains or losses;
- financing proceeds;
- debt service;
- share issuance and dilution;
- restricted or pledged assets; and
- management’s discretionary capital allocation.

A company presentation may emphasize a favorable metric. The filed financial statements and notes reveal the broader structure.

### Governance and disclosure do not equal protocol governance

Boards approve strategy, oversee management, and act within corporate law and governing documents. Shareholders may vote on directors and certain corporate actions. Regulators may require disclosures or impose rules on the company. None of these actors gains authority to change Bitcoin consensus merely because the company owns bitcoin or provides a large service.

A company can influence markets, product access, custody concentration, mining distribution, public narratives, and funding. Those forms of influence can be significant. They remain different from the decentralized process by which software is proposed, reviewed, released, adopted, and enforced by independent participants.

A public company can lobby for policy, fund developers, run nodes, mine blocks, or support a software proposal. Its actions should be evaluated in the role where they occur. Capital allocation is not code review. Code contribution is not adoption. Hashrate is not unilateral rule-making power. A board resolution is not a network upgrade.

### How to read a public company’s Bitcoin claims

Start with the reporting period and legal entity. Then trace each claim to a filing, audited statement, contract, technical repository, or direct operating report.

Ask:

1. Is the claim about holdings, revenue, production, capacity, customers, or a product?
2. What date and reporting boundary apply?
3. Is the number audited, filed, self-reported, estimated, or forward-looking?
4. Does it describe gross activity or net economics?
5. Are customer assets separated from company assets?
6. What custody, debt, collateral, and counterparty relationships exist?
7. Which risks are disclosed but not quantified?
8. What can management change without a shareholder vote?
9. Does the company depend on one custodian, supplier, jurisdiction, pool, data provider, or exchange?
10. Is a corporate policy being confused with a Bitcoin rule?

Public disclosures improve visibility, but they do not eliminate uncertainty. A filing records management’s representations under a legal reporting framework. It can include audited financial statements, unaudited interim information, estimates, risk factors, and forward-looking statements. Readers must preserve those categories.

Public companies participate in Bitcoin through many structures. The most useful analysis does not ask whether a company is “pro-Bitcoin.” It identifies the activity, legal entity, capital structure, custody model, revenue source, dependencies, and limits of authority.

## 3. Key Terms

- **Public company:** Company with securities available in public markets and reporting obligations defined by its jurisdiction and listing structure.
- **Treasury strategy:** Policy for holding and managing corporate assets, including cash, securities, bitcoin, or other investments.
- **Corporate bitcoin:** Bitcoin owned or controlled by a company or consolidated entity rather than held for a customer.
- **Customer assets:** Assets held or administered for clients under a custody, brokerage, trust, or service arrangement.
- **Fair value:** Measurement based on an asset’s market value under an applicable accounting framework.
- **Unrealized gain or loss:** Change in reported value before an asset is sold or otherwise disposed of.
- **Capital structure:** Mix of common equity, preferred securities, debt, cash, and other claims financing a company.
- **Dilution:** Reduction in an existing shareholder’s proportional ownership when additional shares or share-linked securities are issued.
- **Custodian:** Entity responsible under contract and law for safeguarding assets or the credentials that control them.
- **Exchange-traded product:** Publicly traded security or trust interest designed to provide exposure under a defined legal structure.
- **Filed disclosure:** Information submitted to a regulator; evidence of what the filer represented, not automatic proof of operational effectiveness.
- **Protocol authority:** Claimed power to set Bitcoin consensus rules; corporate ownership, market value, or product reach does not provide unilateral protocol authority.
